Direction one

Redline

The plan view of Abu Dhabi property. In surveying, the redline is the boundary drawn around the site being decided on. Here the system is built from plans, measured frames and precise figures, and crimson only ever marks the thing you are looking at.

Display and body

Abu Dhabi property, clearly understood.

One grotesk family with architectural proportions carries display, body and data. Figures are tabular and lining, set from the same family, so prices and areas line up without resorting to monospace.

Why it fits

  • Search, maps and brand share one visual material, so list + map is native rather than bolted on.
  • Measured frames make ordinary listing photos look documented rather than cheap.
  • It extends the existing architectural red mark instead of replacing it.

Watch for

  • Fake technicality: every line and number must measure something real.
  • Drift toward a hairline broadsheet look; line weights must carry meaning.
Direction two

Hard Light

Abu Dhabi as light and shade. Pages move between high-key glare and deep, cool shade, the way the city does at noon and at dusk. Crimson is a solid, matte plane at a few structural moments, never a thin accent.

Display and body

Abu Dhabi, in its own light.

A heavy, compact display grotesk against a quiet text sans. The drama comes from scale contrast, so headlines stay short and body text stays plain.

Shade is a coloured slate-teal, not tinted black. That keeps it out of the "near-black plus one hot accent" look, but only if the discipline holds.

Why it fits

  • Strongest sense of place, rooted in climate and architecture rather than landmarks.
  • Premium presence for developments and international buyers.
  • The richest cinematic motion potential of the three.

Watch for

  • Fails the average-photo test: it needs commissioned photography that is not yet budgeted.
  • Splits into a brand mode and a utility mode on search and forms.
  • Closest to the crowded dark, cinematic luxury genre.
Direction three

Rubric

The reference work for UAE property, in two scripts. Arabic and Latin manuscripts both used red ink to mark headings and what mattered. Here typography is the main material, Arabic and English are set as equals, and crimson marks your place.

Recommendation

Strongest overall

Redline

The only direction where brand and the discovery product use the same visual language, and it survives today's photography.

Safest

Redline or Hard Light

Redline has the lowest delivery risk. Hard Light is least surprising to stakeholders, but safe in the wrong way: closest to the market.

Boldest

Rubric

Bold in strategy, not decoration: Arabic and English as equals from launch, and editorial authority as the brand.

Best balance

Redline, plus two borrowings

Brand, product and award potential in one system.

Recommended: Redline, with exactly two borrowings, each obeying Redline's rules:

  1. From Rubric, bilingual place naming: community and section names in both scripts, so RTL is real from day one without full bilingual content at launch.
  2. From Hard Light, a time-of-day photography brief for community pages only, to add warmth where Redline is weakest.

More borrowing than this blends three systems into one that says nothing.
