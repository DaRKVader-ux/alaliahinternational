# Al Aliah International — Master Brand, Product, Design & Development Context

> **Status:** Primary strategic context for all design and development decisions, unless later project instructions explicitly override it.
> Overrides and resolved decisions are recorded in [`decisions.md`](./decisions.md). Unresolved risks are in [`open-questions.md`](./open-questions.md).

This project is a complete digital redesign and redevelopment of **Al Aliah International**, an Abu Dhabi real-estate brokerage and property-management company.

It is not just a visual redesign. The goal is a distinctive, premium, contemporary, conversion-focused real-estate website that combines:

- strong brand identity
- exceptional editorial art direction
- sophisticated real-estate discovery
- advanced property search
- high-quality motion and interaction
- strong property data architecture
- excellent usability
- high performance
- responsive behavior
- Arabic/RTL readiness
- SEO architecture
- premium visual storytelling

The long-term ambition: a site that could compete aesthetically with award-winning digital experiences while staying practical and commercially effective as a real-estate platform.

- Do not begin by designing random screens.
- Do not begin by copying competitor websites.
- Do not begin by adding animation.

The project must be developed deliberately, in stages.

## 2. The company

- **Company:** Al Aliah International
- **Primary market:** Abu Dhabi, United Arab Emirates
- **Industry:** Real Estate Brokerage / Property Advisory / Real Estate Management

Property categories: residential sales, residential leasing, commercial property, off-plan, apartments, villas, townhouses, penthouses, offices, retail, investment property, property management.

The company has active agents and active inventory across Abu Dhabi. Key areas: Saadiyat Island, Yas Island, Al Reem Island, Al Raha Beach, Al Maryah Island, Khalifa City, Abu Dhabi city, and other major communities.

The business is broader and more established than its current digital presentation suggests. **That gap is one of the main opportunities.**

## 3. Core brand problem

The business is stronger than its presentation. The brand currently reads as a *traditional UAE real-estate company* rather than a *premium contemporary Abu Dhabi property advisory*.

The goal is not to turn Al Aliah into an artificial luxury brand. The goal is for it to feel: established, intelligent, contemporary, trusted, architectural, sophisticated, locally authoritative, internationally credible.

## 4. Desired brand position

**ABU DHABI PROPERTY EXPERTISE.**

Not "We sell properties." Instead: *We help people understand, discover and navigate Abu Dhabi property.*

Evolve from **real-estate agency** to **real-estate advisory + property discovery brand**.

## 5. Internal positioning statement

> Al Aliah is a modern Abu Dhabi property advisory helping people confidently discover, evaluate and secure the right real-estate opportunities.

This is internal framing, not necessarily homepage copy.

## 6. Brand purpose

Make Abu Dhabi real estate easier to understand and easier to navigate.

## 7. Functional promise

Help clients find the right opportunity and guide them through the decision.

## 8. Emotional promise

**CLARITY + CONFIDENCE.** Real-estate decisions involve significant financial commitment, developers, locations, payment plans, investment expectations, market conditions, legal procedures and a lot of information. The brand should reduce uncertainty.

## 9. Brand essence

**CLARITY · ACCESS · CONFIDENCE.** These three concepts apply across design and development.

## 10. Brand archetype

## 13. Brand name

## 14. Logo

The current identity includes a red architectural / skyline mark, uppercase AL ALIAH, INTERNATIONAL, vertical high-rise forms and geometric construction. The concept still carries equity. Do not replace it by default.

**Approach: REFINE RATHER THAN REINVENT.** Possible refinements: simplify geometry, improve spacing, modernize typography, adjust proportions, improve digital performance, create favicon/icon applications, build light and dark versions. Preserve recognizability.

## 15. Color equity

The key brand color is **RED**. It is valuable equity: do not discard it.

Do not default to black + gold, navy + gold, beige + gold, or other generic UAE luxury palettes. Al Aliah's red can be one of its strongest differentiators. The task is to evolve the current corporate red into something more controlled and sophisticated:

**SIGNATURE ARCHITECTURAL CRIMSON.** Red should become recognizably Al Aliah's own color, used deliberately and never excessively.

## 16. Color character

- Red used badly feels promotional, aggressive, discounted, low-end, urgent.
- Red used well feels confident, architectural, decisive, energetic, authoritative, memorable.

## 17. Visual identity direction (directional only, not final)

Possible characteristics: architectural compositions, disciplined typography, large photographic moments, controlled negative space, precise UI, editorial hierarchy, restrained crimson accent, a strong Abu Dhabi sense of place, minimal decorative noise.

## 24. Abu Dhabi as a brand asset

The brand should confidently own Abu Dhabi. Do not position it as another generic *UAE luxury property company*. Position it as **ABU DHABI EXPERTISE WITH UAE REACH**. The city itself is part of the story.

## 25. What the brand must not become

- **A generic luxury fantasy brand.** Avoid overusing gold, marble, yachts, private jets, champagne and fake ultra-luxury imagery.
- **A Property Finder clone.** Users already have portals. Al Aliah's value is expertise, curation, access, advisory and responsiveness.
- **A property developer.** The company represents and brokers property. Do not visually imply it developed every project.
- **A traditional corporate website.** Avoid generic stock photography, corporate gradients, "Welcome to Al Aliah", repetitive service cards, generic mission/vision layouts and dated icon-heavy grids.

## 31. The website is also a product

It is a **PROPERTY DISCOVERY PRODUCT** as well as a brand website. Users must be able to complete real tasks: Buy, Rent, Off-Plan, Commercial, Sell, Invest, Property Management, community discovery, project discovery, developer discovery, agent contact.

## 32. Homepage search is business-critical

Advanced search is one of the most important homepage components and must sit prominently near or inside the hero.

- **Primary intents:** BUY · RENT · OFF-PLAN
- **Possible dimensions:** location, community, building, project, property type, bedrooms, bathrooms, min/max price, min/max size, furnished/unfurnished, completion status, ready/off-plan, developer, amenities, sea view, waterfront, balcony, parking, pool, payment plan, handover year
- Do not expose every filter in the initial interface. **Progressive disclosure is preferred.**

## 33. Natural-language search

A future or advanced version should handle queries like:

> 2 bedroom waterfront apartment on Reem under AED 1.8M with balcony

and translate them into structured filters: `Buy` · `Al Reem` · `Apartment` · `2 Bedrooms` · `≤ AED 1.8M` · `Waterfront` · `Balcony`.

This must be a useful discovery tool, not an AI gimmick.

## 34. Search results experience: LIST + MAP

**Desktop:** property cards, map, synchronized pins, sticky filters, instant filtering, sorting, pagination or progressive loading, favorite/save.

**Mobile:** card-first experience, floating Map button, bottom-sheet filters, sticky search/filter controls. Do not just compress the desktop layout.

## 35. Property card principles

Prioritize: high-quality image, purpose/status, price, property name, community, beds, baths, area, favorite/save. Do not overload cards. Secondary information belongs deeper in the experience.

## 36. Property detail experience

Possible hierarchy: immersive gallery → price → title → community → facts → save → share → WhatsApp → viewing request → description → features → floor plan → location → payment plan → project info → developer → community info → agent → related properties.

**Off-plan also needs:** developer, handover, payment plan, down payment, unit types, floor plans, brochure, project amenities, investment context.

## 45. Trust system

## 46. Design philosophy

Balance **EMOTION** with **UTILITY**. Brand sections can be expressive; search and listing interfaces must be highly usable. Do not trade usability for Awwwards-style effects.

## 47. Core aesthetic idea (working territory, not an approved system)

**ARCHITECTURAL EDITORIAL PROPERTY PLATFORM.** Themes: architecture, structure, negative space, typography, photography, Abu Dhabi, controlled crimson, data clarity.

## 48. Design anti-patterns

Avoid: excessive rounded cards · generic SaaS aesthetics · excessive glassmorphism · neon gradients · meaningless gradients · excessive blur · gold-luxury clichés · random dark mode · generic AI imagery · generic skyscraper CGI · excessive card grids · endless logo strips · massive testimonial sliders · oversized empty typography with no purpose · unreadable ultra-light fonts · overly decorative serif use · arbitrary horizontal scrolling · scroll hijacking · excessive custom cursor behavior · long intro/loading screens · animation on every element · pointless 3D.

## 49. Motion principle (applies at Stage 04)

**ARCHITECTURE MOVES SLOWLY. UI MOVES QUICKLY.** Large visual moments may feel cinematic; functional UI must stay responsive.

| Interaction | Approximate range |
|---|---|
| UI interactions | 150–250 ms |
| Hover states | 150–300 ms |
| Section transitions | 500–800 ms |
| Large image movement | 700–1200 ms |

These are conceptual ranges, not fixed rules. Motion must stay performant.

## 50. Possible interaction territories (later)

Cinematic hero media, controlled parallax, typography masking, community transitions, map/property synchronization, fluid gallery transitions, image reveals, structured page transitions, navigation expansion, subtle red interaction cues, filter micro-interactions, intelligent search transitions.

Do not add any of these just because they look impressive. Every interaction needs a reason.

## 51. Performance

Responsive images · modern image formats · lazy loading · controlled video use · preload strategy · reduced motion · mobile performance · good Core Web Vitals · minimal JavaScript · no unnecessary animation libraries.

## 52. Accessibility

Meaningful semantic structure · usable keyboard navigation · visible focus states · good text contrast · accessible forms · meaningful labels · accessible search/filter behavior · reduced-motion support · appropriate target sizes. Aesthetic choices must never make content inaccessible.

## 53. Technical direction

The preferred visual layer may be **FRAMER**, but do not force every system into native Framer CMS if that creates poor data architecture.

Framer would mainly handle: brand experience, homepage, editorial pages, communities, projects, agent pages, content, motion, presentation. Advanced property search may need a stronger external data layer.

## 54. Possible backend

**Supabase / PostgreSQL** for property data. Candidate tables: `properties`, `projects`, `developers`, `communities`, `agents`, `property_types`, `amenities`, `property_amenities`, `media`, `project_units`, `leads`, `saved_properties`.

## 55. Property data

Candidate fields: id, slug, title, purpose, status, price, currency, bedrooms, bathrooms, size, property type, community, building, developer, latitude, longitude, furnished, completion status, handover, featured, agent, description, amenities, gallery, floor plans.

**The schema must match Al Aliah's actual data source. Do not invent a final schema before understanding their current CRM/feed.**

## 63. Current project status

Stage 01 brand audit is established. Do not prematurely finalize the homepage design, typography, final colors, layouts, animations, motion, component styling or page transitions.

**Next: STAGE 02, the visual world / vibe / aesthetic system.** Develop several considered directions. For each, explain: central concept, mood, typography, color behavior, imagery, composition, UI characteristics, level of luxury, architectural influence, editorial influence, strengths, risks, relevance to Al Aliah, and differentiation from competitors.

Do not propose a direction because it looks fashionable. Every choice must trace back to the brand strategy.

## 64. Working principle: the ten questions

For every major design decision, ask:

1. Does this feel like Al Aliah?
2. Does it reinforce Abu Dhabi expertise?
3. Does it make the property experience clearer?
4. Does it increase confidence?
5. Does it differentiate Al Aliah?
6. Does it work for both premium property and normal inventory?
7. Is it usable on mobile?
8. Does it scale across hundreds or thousands of properties?
9. Can it work in Arabic?
10. Is the interaction worth its performance cost?

If any answer is no, rethink it.

## 65. Final project north star

The finished experience should feel **architectural, editorial, intelligent, precise, premium, restrained, human, distinctly Abu Dhabi, and recognizably Al Aliah**, while still being an extremely effective platform for searching, discovering, evaluating, contacting, buying, renting, selling and investing in property.
