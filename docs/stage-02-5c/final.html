1. Typography

Long names, held

Abu Dhabi place names run from "Yas Island" to "Mohammed Bin Zayed City". Two display systems are tested against T1 at real sizes in real slots. Switch the system to reset this page's own type too.

Body text in the same family at normal width. Long community and project names are the hardest case for a compressed display face: they either wrap badly, shrink until they lose authority, or get squeezed past legibility.

Each column applies the same fit rule at the same slot width. "Overflows" marks a name the rule could not hold.

1. Bind particles"Al", "Bin" and "Abu" never end a line: "Madinat / Al Riyad", not "Madinat Al / Riyad".
2. Balance two linesA display name may take two balanced lines at full size. Never three.
3. Step down onceNames of 19 characters or more drop one scale step. No further.
4. Arabic rides the last lineThe Arabic name sits on the final Latin baseline, end-aligned, at 0.42 of the Latin size. Arabic names over 14 characters take their own line below, still end-aligned.

Recommendation: T5, Sofia Sans, as the working type system for Stage 03

Measured at 100 px, the ten names average 460 px wide in T1, 465 in T5 and 541 in T4. T1 and T5 hold all ten in one line at this slot (the two longest one step down); T4 has to break "Saadiyat Island" and "Madinat Al Riyad". So compression was never T1's problem. Character is: Anybody's compressed letterforms turn quirky over four words, and its normal width is not a calm reading face.

T5 keeps T1's compression and authority, reads plainly at length, and its text face is quiet. The cost: Sofia Sans has no extended width, so labels move from "extended" to normal width at a smaller size. Compressed still means place or figure; normal means reading and labels. T4 is the fallback if extended labels matter more than compression.

Not final for production: a licensed grotesk with a true compressed width (Stage 05, budget) can replace it without changing the roles.

2. Signature card

Site select, refined

The card carries its site. A site strip from the map sits inside every card, and hovering or focusing a card draws the redline around its community in the strip and on the map at the same moment. The card opens the listing; Shortlist is its own control.

Stylised geography. Pins sit at community level until listings carry real coordinates (open question Q3).

3. Map art direction

A Redline cartography

From schematic to system: a fixed layer order, a coastal shelf instead of flat water, a three-step road hierarchy, a three-step label hierarchy, and symbols that hold their size at every zoom. Production applies this as a MapLibre style on vector tiles.

Layers

Stylised geography for art direction: positions are approximate and not for navigation. Production geometry comes from the tile source (decision D-006).

Layer order, bottom to top

  1. WaterPale slate, flat
  2. Coastal shelfTwo depth bands follow every coast
  3. LandSheet, with a hairline coast
  4. Open space and fabricNeutral tints, no green; building fabric from community zoom
  5. RoadsMotorway and arterial, cased in white; minor roads at street zoom
